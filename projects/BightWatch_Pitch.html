BightWatch

A climate-adaptive risk-monitoring tool for California's Marine Protected Area network — so a small team with 124 MPAs can decide, every quarter, which zones of coastline need the closest attention next.

We rank, we don't regulate. BightWatch scores the 13 CalCOFI zones inside the Southern California Bight for how much climate stress has built up, and suggests where the next cruise or sampling effort will do the most good.
§ 02 · WHO THIS IS FOR

We built this for the people doing the real work.

MPA managers and policy analysts who have to watch a lot of coastline with a finite team. Everything we designed starts from their day.

A Senior Environmental Scientist at CDFW Marine Region's MPA Management Program.
Or, in parallel: an MPA Policy Analyst at the California Ocean Protection Council.
We are here to support the hard work they already do — not to replace their judgment, and not to sell them a forecast.

A small storyboard of her quarter.

So judges can see the exact moment BightWatch fits into her work.

Step 1 · Monday morning
She opens six different dashboards to plan this quarter's cruise.
HABMAP pier samples, SCCOOS and CeNCOOS buoys, CalCOFI cruise records (last bottle-DB update: May 2021), NOAA OISST satellite tiles, internal agency briefings. Not one of them tells her which zones need a boat first. She decides in her head.
This is the daily reality we want to make easier.
Step 2 · Why now
The 2023 Decadal Review named the gap we are trying to fill.
Every ten years, OPC and CDFW publish a joint review of the MPA network. The 2023 report specifically flagged "climate-adaptive monitoring prioritization" as an unmet need. That is the seat we are sitting in. The next review window closes around 2028.
Her deadline is a real, published agency document.
Step 3 · What's at stake
Without better triage, big events catch the team late.
When the 2014–16 North Pacific "Blob" marine heatwave hit, a toxic algal bloom closed California's Dungeness crab fishery and CDFW spent roughly $2M on an emergency Blob Response. Catching that kind of signal a quarter earlier is the cost saving we care about.
Earlier attention = smaller emergency later.
§ 03 · THE OPERATIONAL QUESTION

Which zones should we watch more closely next quarter — and why?

The MPA network is 124 areas. A finite team cannot deeply monitor all of them every quarter. Triage is the real job.

What the question is not

  • Not a prediction of next month's HAB.
  • Not a recommendation to change fishing rules.
  • Not a climate attribution study.
  • Not a substitute for the MPA scientist's judgment — an input to it.

What the question is

  • Where has upper-ocean stress drifted furthest from this zone's own baseline?
  • Which of those zones also overlap an active MPA or a high-fishing-pressure pocket?
  • Which is a next-cruise-worth adjustment — not a policy change?
Our True North metric — in plain English

Allocation-cycle influence rate = the fraction of quarters where BightWatch's top-ranked zones received above-average monitoring effort the next quarter.

Said another way: of the eight quarters in a two-year pilot, how many times did the tool actually change where the team sent a boat?

Target ≥ 3 out of 8 (≈ 38%) — that's how we define success. Floor ≥ 1 out of 8 — below that, we'd rather stop the pilot than pretend. We don't optimize for prediction accuracy, subscriber count, or HAB recall. We optimize for whether the tool gets used.

§ 04 · COMPETITIVE LANDSCAPE

The space is crowded. The specific seat is empty.

There are world-class tools for HAB forecasting, ecosystem status, and biodiversity — but no one ranks the 13 CalCOFI-SCB zones for cruise-cycle triage.

Existing tool Does really well Why it does not replace us
C-HARM (NOAA/NCCOS) HAB forecast (Pseudo-nitzschia, domoic acid) at 3km. Species-specific, not zone-specific; doesn't speak cruise allocation.
CA HAB Bulletin (CDPH / SCCOOS) Public shellfish-safety bulletin. Consumer-facing; does not rank monitoring zones.
CCIEA (NOAA) Annual CCS ecosystem status, publication-grade. Annual cadence, basin scale — not the quarterly cruise cycle.
MLPA monitoring (PISCO, MARINe, Reef Check) Site-level biology / biodiversity time-series. Site-level, not zone-level; no climate-stress ranking.
HABMAP Weekly pier samples for HAB species. Pier-point, not spatial; no decision layer.
The unoccupied seat

Zone-scale, cruise-cycle, climate-adaptive risk monitoring with a named action recommendation per zone. That's us. Everyone else is adjacent.

§ 06 · WHAT WE BUILT

Four steps. One screen. One decision.

The scientist opens one page on Monday morning. That's the product.

🌊
Ingest
CalCOFI bottle DB, OISST bridge, BEUTI, MPA polygons, fishing-pressure proxy.
📐
Score
Per-zone climate-stress risk score from warming + anomaly signals, robust-linear baseline.
📋
Rank
Ordered queue of 13 zones, each with a confidence badge so the team sees how much to trust the ranking.
🎯
Act
Named action per zone: investigate_now / schedule_next_cruise / hold.

One screen, three columns

  • Risk Map — the 13 zones, color-coded by their monitoring-priority risk score.
  • Zone card — risk score, overlap with named MPAs, fishing pressure, confidence badge, and a short source-grounded summary.
  • Action queue — the ranked list, one named action per row.

One tone

  • We surface risk per zone, transparently — we don't hide it behind euphemisms.
  • Every number on the card traces back to a CSV cell — no free-form LLM guessing.
  • No number without a source; no action without a justification; no ranking without a confidence badge.

The honesty beat

R2 on the 2014–2021 holdout is −0.68. That's worse than predicting the mean. We disclose it on the same screen as the ranking. The ranking is useful; the prediction is not. We'd rather say that up front than have a reviewer find it later.

§ 09 · OUR STANCE

We're trying to be useful to the people doing the work.

A small, honest tool that helps a real team make a recurring quarterly decision a little bit better.

What we will do

  • Ship a ranked risk queue of the 13 CalCOFI-SCB zones with a named action per row.
  • Publish the confidence-badge table on the same screen.
  • Cite every number to a CSV cell. Every time.
  • Use source-grounded Gemini — a templater, not a generator.
  • Disclose what our model can't do, on the ranking page, not in a footnote.

What we will not do

  • Not predict individual HAB events — C-HARM already does that well.
  • Not recommend regulatory closures — that's CDPH's role.
  • Not market to recreational users — wrong trust model.
  • Not paper over weak R2 — honesty is the moat.
  • Not replace the MPA scientist's judgment — we augment it for one decision per quarter.
Why we're doing this

The MPA scientists we spoke with don't need another dashboard. They need a clearer, more defensible answer to one quarterly question. BightWatch is our small contribution to a large, important job — we want to do it carefully and honestly.

§ 11 · GLOSSARY

Terms used in this pitch — searchable.

Hover any underlined term above for the short definition; use the search box below for the full list.

MPA — Marine Protected Area. A designated area of the ocean with specific rules limiting extraction and protecting habitat. California has 124 MPAs under the MLPA network.
MLPA — Marine Life Protection Act. California 1999 law that established the state's MPA network; monitored jointly by CDFW, OPC, PISCO, Reef Check, and MARINe.
CDFW — California Department of Fish & Wildlife. State agency that operates the MPA network. Our primary buyer channel is CDFW Marine Region's MPA Management Program.
OPC — California Ocean Protection Council. State policy body that sets MPA policy and co-owns the Decadal Review with CDFW. Our parallel buyer channel.
CCC — California Coastal Commission. Agency with jurisdiction over California's coastal zone; an adjacent buyer.
WCRO — NOAA West Coast Regional Office. Federal sub-region within NMFS; an adjacent public-sector buyer.
NMS — National Marine Sanctuary. Federal sanctuary system (e.g. Channel Islands NMS). Potential adjacent buyer.
CDPH — California Department of Public Health. Owns shellfish-safety closures. We do not sell to CDPH.
CalCOFI — California Cooperative Oceanic Fisheries Investigations. Joint CA/NOAA/Scripps program running quarterly cruises since 1949. Our core bottle-database source (through May 2021).
SCB — Southern California Bight. The curved coastal region from Point Conception to the US-Mexico border. We operate on the 13 CalCOFI zones inside the SCB.
Zone — CalCOFI grid cell. We use the "line × station" grid naming (e.g. Z_83.3_offshore). 13 zones are inside our SCB frame.
OISST — Optimum Interpolation Sea Surface Temperature. NOAA daily 0.25° satellite SST product; our warming-signal source between CalCOFI cruises.
BEUTI — Biologically Effective Upwelling Transport Index. Daily upwelling index (Jacox et al.); our ocean-dynamics proxy for nutrient pulses.
HABMAP — HAB Monitoring & Alert Program. SCCOOS-partnered weekly pier-sample network for toxic algae along CA coast.
HAB — Harmful Algal Bloom. Rapid growth of toxin-producing algae (e.g. Pseudo-nitzschia). Economic and public-health risk.
DA — Domoic Acid. Neurotoxin produced by Pseudo-nitzschia blooms. Causes shellfish closures and crab-fishery disruption; 2015 CA event was the benchmark.
Pseudo-nitzschia. Diatom genus responsible for most domoic-acid events in the CA Current System.
MHW — Marine Heatwave. Prolonged period of anomalously warm sea temperature. The 2014–16 North Pacific "Blob" MHW is our benchmark climate-stress event.
The Blob. Informal name for the 2014–16 North Pacific MHW. Triggered the 2015 DA bloom and CDFW Blob Response (~$2M spend).
SCCOOS — Southern California Coastal Ocean Observing System. IOOS Regional Association; operates many of the feeds we ingest.
CeNCOOS — Central and Northern California Ocean Observing System. Sister IOOS RA to SCCOOS, covering the CA coast north of Point Conception.
IOOS — Integrated Ocean Observing System. NOAA-led federated system of 11 Regional Associations. Sub-award ceiling ~$40K/yr regionally, ~$440–660K nationally.
C-HARM. California Harmful Algae Risk Mapping — NOAA/NCCOS forecast for Pseudo-nitzschia / domoic acid. Adjacent tool, not competitor.
CCIEA — California Current Integrated Ecosystem Assessment. NOAA's annual ecosystem-status report for the CA Current System. Annual cadence; we work at quarterly cadence.
CCS — California Current System. The eastern-boundary current system off the US West Coast; the geographic context of CCIEA.
PISCO / MARINe / Reef Check. Long-running MPA biological monitoring networks operating at the site level. We operate at the zone level.
NCCOS — NOAA National Centers for Coastal Ocean Science. Parent unit for C-HARM.
Decadal Review. The joint OPC+CDFW 10-year review of California's MPA network. 2023 cycle flagged climate-adaptive monitoring prioritization as an unmet need — our market signal. Next cycle ~2028.
SCSR — Scientific & Statistical Review. Sub-component of Decadal Review focused on monitoring methodology.
True North metric. The single measure we optimize for: allocation-cycle influence rate — the fraction of quarters where BightWatch's top-ranked zones got above-mean monitoring effort the next quarter. Target ≥ 3/8; floor ≥ 1/8.
Allocation cycle. A single quarterly monitoring-planning cycle run by the MPA Management Program. 8 per 2-year pilot.
Monitoring Priority / Risk Score. The product-facing name for our per-zone risk ranking metric. Internally in the CSV it is zone_risk_score (we kept the filename stable to not break the chart pack).
Confidence badge. A–F grade assigned to each hypothesis / capability based on how well the analysis meets our rigor gates. Generated from outputs/thread_b/confidence_badges.csv, not hand-assigned.
R2 (coefficient of determination). Standard regression fit statistic. R2 > 0 means the model beats the mean; R2 < 0 means it's worse than predicting the mean. Our holdout is R2 = −0.68, which we disclose.
Holdout / train split. Train ≤ 2013; holdout 2014–2021. Holdout is the honesty test — it sees data the model did not train on.
Robust-linear model. A linear regression fitted with an M-estimator (Huber loss) rather than OLS, so outliers do not dominate the fit. Our B5 baseline.
Simpson's paradox. A statistical effect where a pattern at the aggregate level reverses inside subgroups. We run a segment-first check (our "A5 gate") before publishing any aggregate finding.
A5 gate. Our internal Simpson's-paradox check — every all-zone conclusion must be reproduced at the per-zone level, or the conclusion is marked unresolved.
Source-grounded LLM. An LLM used strictly as a templater over structured CSV output. Every rendered number must match a CSV cell; if not, the sentence falls back to a neutral template. We use Gemini this way.
Free-form LLM (cut). A generative LLM that produces text without requiring every number to trace to a source. We do not ship this.
Zone action mapping. The rule that maps a zone's risk score and context to one of three named actions: investigate_now, schedule_next_cruise, hold.
Design partnership. A paid 1-agency Y1 engagement where the user co-designs the product with us and commits to eight quarterly evaluation cycles. Not a pilot-for-free; not priced equity.
Sub-award (IOOS). A federal pass-through grant from IOOS to a Regional Association (e.g. SCCOOS). Ceiling per RA is ~$40K/yr — which is why SCCOOS is a partner, not a primary buyer.
TAM — Total Addressable Market. Upper bound of revenue in a defined market. Ours: $400–700K/yr across CDFW + OPC + CCC + WCRO + NMS.
Budget envelope. The plausible spend range a public-sector buyer could authorize without a special procurement process. Our Y1 envelope is $30–100K.
ERDDAP. A data-server standard used across IOOS for programmatic access to oceanographic datasets.
Bottle database (CalCOFI). The discrete-sample water chemistry and zooplankton table from CalCOFI cruises — the core of our feature store. Cutoff: May 2021.
Fishing pressure proxy (0–100). Our zone-level index combining published CDFW commercial-landings zones. Z_86.7_inshore (Santa Monica Bay) is 100.
Priority queue. The ranked list of all 13 zones with per-zone risk score, action, and confidence badge — produced from zone_action_queue.csv.
